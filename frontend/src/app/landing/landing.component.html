INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY — PATENTS, INDUSTRIAL DESIGNS AND UTILITY MODELS
CLASS ONE — BACKGROUND AND INTRODUCTION TO PATENTS

Patents originated from the industrial society in England. Their roots can be traced to the medieval period through Letters Patent — open letters issued by the King under the royal seal.

Letters Patent granted special rights and privileges, especially to foreign craftsmen, weavers, and skilled artisans. Early patents did not require invention or novelty; the focus was on trade promotion and royal favour.

In 1311, a patent was granted to John Kempe, who was allowed to practice weaving in England despite guild restrictions.

Temporary presence of patented products on foreign aircraft, ships, and vehicles does not infringe.

Prior user rights — a person who used the invention before patent filing, or made serious preparations, may continue using it. The right only covers prior acts already undertaken.

How do patents link with shipping?
The documents connect the two through "temporary presence" — patented products on foreign ships or vehicles do not constitute infringement.
